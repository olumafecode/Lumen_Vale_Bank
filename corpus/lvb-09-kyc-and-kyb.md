## 1. Customer identification and business understanding
### 1.1 Purpose and scope
Before activating a customer relationship, LVB verifies who the customer is, why the service is needed, and what activity is reasonably expected. Know Your Customer (KYC) covers individuals and their authorized representatives. Know Your Customer's Business (KYB) covers business identity, ownership, control, and expected operations. This is a fictional due-diligence framework, not a statement of legal anti-money-laundering requirements.

The onboarding employee collects approved evidence, records verification results, and assigns the case for independent review. The Branch Manager checks completeness, while Compliance decides high-risk cases and unresolved screening concerns. No employee may activate an account using their own approval of incomplete evidence. Revenue targets and personal familiarity do not justify bypassing the process.

### 1.2 Individual customers
Record the customer's legal name, date of birth, residential address, valid identity evidence, purpose of the account, occupation or activity, and expected source and pattern of funds. Verify identity and address through approved independent sources. An image of a document alone is not automatically sufficient: record the approved verification method, result, reviewer, and date. Resolve inconsistencies before activation.

For someone acting for another person, verify the representative's identity and documented authority, as well as the customer's required information. Do not accept a relative's informal statement as authority. If evidence is unavailable or unreliable, pause onboarding and ask Compliance for an approved alternative or a decline decision. This corpus does not enumerate acceptable real-world identity-document types.

### 1.3 Business customers
Record legal and trading names, registration evidence, registered and operating addresses, nature of business, expected transaction activity, source of funds, authorized signatories, ownership structure, and natural persons who ultimately own or control the business. Verify the entity and signatory authority using approved independent evidence. Understand what the business sells, its principal customer and supplier types, and why the requested services fit that activity. A registration certificate alone does not complete KYB.

## 2. Ownership, risk assessment, and approval
### 2.1 Beneficial ownership
For this fictional policy, identify and verify each natural person who directly or indirectly owns 25% or more of the business, plus any person exercising control through other means. Exactly 25% qualifies. Trace ownership through intermediate entities; do not stop at a company name. If no natural person meets the ownership threshold, identify and verify the senior managing official and document the ownership and control assessment. This fallback must not conceal an unresolved or opaque ownership chain.

If reliable ownership or control information cannot be obtained, do not activate the relationship. Compliance decides whether more evidence can resolve the issue or onboarding must be declined. The 25% threshold is an invented internal rule for the project and is not presented as the law of any country.

### 2.2 Risk assessment and screening
Assess customer activity, ownership complexity, expected transaction pattern, delivery channel, and inconsistencies in evidence. Use the approved screening process for customers, relevant owners, and signatories. A possible match is unresolved until Compliance reviews identifiers and evidence. Staff must not label someone sanctioned or suspicious solely because a name resembles another entry, and must not override a match to finish onboarding.

Low-risk cases have verified straightforward identity and activity. Medium-risk cases require additional explanation or monitoring but have resolved evidence. High-risk cases include materially complex ownership or other concerns requiring enhanced review. An unresolved screening match or unverifiable identity blocks activation; it is not cured simply by assigning a high-risk rating.

### 2.3 Enhanced due diligence
For a high-risk case, obtain additional corroboration of source of funds, source of wealth where relevant to the assessed risk, ownership, business purpose, and expected activity. Source of funds describes where money for the relationship comes from; source of wealth describes how overall wealth was accumulated. Record the rationale and monitoring plan. Both the Compliance Officer and Head of Operations must approve a high-risk relationship before activation. Neither may approve their own prepared assessment without an independent review. This approval cannot waive missing identity or unresolved screening evidence.

## 3. Ongoing review, concerns, and records
### 3.1 Review schedule and triggers
Review low-risk relationships every 36 months, medium-risk every 24 months, and high-risk every 12 months from the last completed review or initial approval. A calendar reminder alone is not a completed review. Confirm that identity, ownership, authorized signatories, purpose, and expected activity remain accurate, and record the evidence and next due date.

Changes in ownership, signatories, business activity, transaction pattern, or credible adverse information trigger a review without waiting for the periodic due date. The employee who notices the change refers it to Compliance by the end of the next business day. Compliance assesses urgency and records required restrictions and an action plan within two business days of referral. If active fraud or a security incident is suspected, the immediate specialist reporting routes apply as well.

### 3.2 Unusual activity and confidentiality
Record unusual activity facts in the restricted Customer Review queue and notify Compliance by the end of the same business day. If there is active suspected fraud or imminent loss, notify Compliance and Operations immediately. Do not independently tell the customer that an internal suspicion report or investigation exists, promise that activity is lawful, or advise how to avoid a threshold.

Compliance evaluates the facts, decides further internal action, and records any external reporting assessment. This corpus does not define statutory reporting forms, regulator identities, filing deadlines, or legal tipping-off rules. Staff must not invent these details. An unusual transaction is a reason for assessment, not proof of wrongdoing.

### 3.3 Retention and examples
Retain KYC and KYB evidence for seven years after the customer relationship ends; transactions follow their separate transaction-date schedule in LVB-06. Restrict records to authorized onboarding, review, and oversight staff. A hold suspends normal deletion. Corrections preserve original evidence and the reason for change rather than silently replacing a prior record.

Example: a business has four equal natural-person owners. All four meet the 25% threshold and must be identified and verified. Example: a low-risk customer's business changes substantially six months after review; staff trigger a new assessment rather than waiting 36 months. Example: a registration certificate with no verified ownership or signatory authority is insufficient for activation.
