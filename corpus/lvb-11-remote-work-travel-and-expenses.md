## 1. Remote-work eligibility and safeguards
### 1.1 Eligibility and approval
Employees whose duties can be performed securely away from a branch may request up to two remote-working days per week. Branch cash-handling duties are not eligible while assigned to cash service. Eligibility is not automatic: the line manager approves coverage and performance arrangements, and People Operations records the arrangement in StaffHub before it starts. The approved arrangement identifies location, days, review date, and contact expectations.

Normal remote arrangements last up to three months before review. A manager may change the schedule for documented service or security reasons after discussing it with the employee. Remote work does not expand working hours, create extra leave, or authorize work from an unapproved location. This corpus does not authorize cross-border working; refer such requests to People Operations for a separate assessment.

### 1.2 Security and workspace
Use a bank-managed device, approved remote connection, MFA, and a private workspace that prevents others seeing or hearing customer information. Personal devices must not access Confidential or Restricted bank data. Do not print Restricted information at home, let household members use the bank device, or transfer files to personal storage. Lock the screen whenever leaving it and keep equipment secure between sessions.

Avoid public Wi-Fi. If an approved task must be done away from the approved workspace, obtain manager approval and use a bank-approved connection such as a managed hotspot; never disable security controls to make a connection work. A lost device or suspected disclosure is reported within 15 minutes under LVB-02, including outside normal office hours through the on-call route.

### 1.3 Availability and disruption
Be available during the agreed work schedule and update the approved calendar. For a connection outage, notify the manager promptly, stop work that cannot be completed securely, and agree a return-to-office or alternative assignment. Do not use an unapproved personal account to keep a task moving. The manager records any change that affects coverage or working arrangements.

## 2. Travel authorization and reimbursable expenses
### 2.1 Approval before spending
Business travel requires written line-manager approval in StaffHub before booking. Record the purpose, dates, destination, expected cost, and cost center. If the estimated total trip cost exceeds CU 1,000, also obtain Finance Controller approval before booking. Exactly CU 1,000 does not trigger the additional amount-based trip approval. Travelers must not divide a booking to avoid the threshold.

Where the line manager is the traveler, approval goes to their next-level manager. A Finance Controller's own trip requiring Finance-level approval is routed to the CEO and an independent Finance reviewer. Emergency travel still needs an authorized written approval before commitment; urgency does not create self-approval. The bank may decline reimbursement for spending outside approved scope, subject to documented review.

### 2.2 Eligible costs and limits
Reimburse reasonable, necessary business costs supported by receipts: approved transport, accommodation, meals during approved business travel, and approved business supplies. Hotel reimbursement is capped at CU 150 per night excluding separately itemized mandatory lodging taxes. Meals are capped at CU 40 per travel day including taxes and tips. These are reimbursement ceilings, not automatic allowances; claim actual eligible spending.

Alcohol, entertainment unrelated to the approved purpose, personal purchases, penalties, companion travel, and ordinary commuting are not reimbursable. Personal vehicle business mileage is reimbursed at CU 0.30 per kilometer using an approved route log; ordinary commuting is excluded. Staff must not also claim fuel for the same mileage. Remote-working days do not automatically qualify for internet, furniture, or utility reimbursement.

### 2.3 Above-limit requests
A hotel or meal expense above its cap needs prior written Finance Controller approval, with the reason and approved maximum recorded in StaffHub. Approval of the trip alone does not waive a category cap. If an unexpected essential cost could not reasonably be preapproved, explain it promptly; Finance assesses it individually and reimbursement is not guaranteed. Gift and hospitality restrictions in LVB-07 still apply to spending claimed as a business expense.

## 3. Claims, review, and exceptions
### 3.1 Submission and evidence
Submit an expense claim within 15 calendar days after the trip ends or, for a non-travel expense, after the purchase date. Attach itemized receipts, business purpose, dates, cost center, prior approvals, and a mileage log where applicable. If a receipt is missing, provide a signed explanation and alternative evidence for Finance review; a missing receipt is not automatically approved or automatically evidence of misconduct.

The line manager checks business purpose and approved scope. Finance checks calculations, category limits, duplicate claims, and evidence. An expense claim totaling more than CU 500 requires Finance Controller approval in addition to manager approval before payment. Exactly CU 500 does not trigger this extra claim-total approval. A CU 900 trip may therefore need no additional trip-budget approval but still need Finance Controller approval for its claim. Trip and claim thresholds govern different steps.

### 3.2 Payment and correction
Finance targets payment within ten business days after all required approvals and complete evidence are received. The clock does not start with an incomplete first submission. If information is missing, Finance returns the claim with a specific explanation and records the remaining requirement. Employees may correct a claim without altering the original receipt or hiding a prior submission.

Late claims require a reason and Finance Controller review; the policy provides no automatic late-claim entitlement. Employees must not approve their own expenses at any level. An approver's own claim goes to the next-level manager and an independent Finance approver; CEO claims go to the Board Audit Committee chair and an independent Finance reviewer. Suspected fabricated or duplicate claims follow LVB-07 and, where appropriate, LVB-03.

### 3.3 Records and examples
Keep expense claims and supporting records for seven years after the relevant financial year-end under LVB-06. Remote-work approvals are personnel records retained for six years after departure. People Operations and Operations review remote arrangements at least every three months; Finance reviews expense exceptions quarterly for repeated issues.

Example: CU 32 in eligible meals yields CU 32 reimbursement, not the CU 40 cap. Example: a teller assigned to cash service cannot perform that duty remotely. Example: a claim with CU 480 in hotel charges and CU 60 in eligible meals totals CU 540 and needs the additional claim-total approval, even when each category is within its daily cap.
