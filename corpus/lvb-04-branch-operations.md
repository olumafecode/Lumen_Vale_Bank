## 1. Opening, staffing, and customer service
### 1.1 Opening readiness
Branch customer hours are 09:00-16:00 on business days. Before opening, two authorized staff complete and sign the opening checklist: premises safe to enter, security systems available, workstations ready, cash records available, and required staffing present. Detailed alarm procedures, combinations, and physical-security layouts are deliberately excluded from this academic corpus.

At least two authorized staff must be present for cash-service opening. If this condition is not met, the Branch Manager delays cash service, informs the Head of Operations, records the reason, and arranges safe customer communication. No employee may conduct a dual-control task alone to avoid a queue. The manager must not pressure an employee to certify a check they did not perform.

### 1.2 Identity and service boundaries
Before disclosing account information or processing a transaction, staff complete the approved customer-verification workflow and record its result. A familiar face or colleague's recognition does not replace the workflow. New accounts and changes that require due diligence follow LVB-09; branch staff cannot waive missing verification documents. Customer-identification data must not be copied into ordinary chat or personal notes.

Handle customers respectfully and provide reasonable service alternatives within available procedures. Do not discuss account balances where other customers can hear. If identity is uncertain, pause the requested disclosure or transaction and refer to the Branch Manager. Do not tell the customer that an account is suspicious or reveal an internal investigation.

### 1.3 Dual control and allocation
Each teller uses an individually assigned till and login. Transfers into or out of shared cash custody require two authorized staff to verify the amount and record both identities. Record a transfer at the time it occurs, not at the end of the day from memory. Neither person may sign for the other. Cash-service supervision and financial review must remain separate where required by LVB-08.

## 2. Transaction controls and discrepancy response
### 2.1 Fictional approval thresholds
A cash payout of more than CU 5,000 requires Branch Manager approval before release, in addition to normal verification. A payout of exactly CU 5,000 does not trigger this additional amount-based approval. If the manager initiated the transaction, an authorized independent deputy or the Head of Operations approves it instead. Any suspicious pattern must be referred regardless of value.

All manual account adjustments require an independent checker before posting. The preparer records the reason, supporting evidence, affected account reference, and proposed amount. The checker verifies the evidence and authority. Staff must not split a transaction or adjustment to avoid a threshold, use a customer's identity to test a system, or approve their own work. These are internal example controls, not statutory thresholds.

### 2.2 Cash differences
Count and reconcile each till at close of cash service. If a difference exists, the teller and an independent checker recount once and compare authorized transaction records. Record every unresolved difference, regardless of amount, in the cash-discrepancy log on the same day. A difference exceeding CU 100 must be escalated to the Head of Operations within one hour of discovery; any suspected fraud must be escalated immediately regardless of amount.

Do not conceal a shortage with personal money, transfer a difference to another till, or alter a transaction record without the formal adjustment process. Preserve the original totals and add an explanatory correction with author and timestamp. If misconduct is suspected, use LVB-03; if a security event is suspected, the 15-minute reporting deadline in LVB-02 also applies.

### 2.3 Complaints and interruptions
Record customer complaints in CaseTrack on the day received and send them to the Customer Service lead. Acknowledge within two business days and provide a resolution or progress update within ten business days. A complaint involving privacy, suspected fraud, or employee misconduct also follows the relevant specialist policy. During system downtime, do not take customer credentials or make unauthorized offline postings; follow section 3.1.

## 3. Closing, continuity, and review
### 3.1 Service outage
The Branch Manager records the outage and notifies the Service Desk and Head of Operations. Stop transactions whose identity, balance, approval, or audit checks cannot be completed. Do not write passwords or account details on an improvised paper queue. Customer contact details collected for a callback must use an approved minimal-data form stored securely and entered into the approved system after restoration.

The Head of Operations decides whether limited services can continue under an existing approved continuity procedure. This corpus does not authorize an offline cash-payment process. Staff give the approved factual service message and available alternatives without speculation about the cause. A suspected cyber incident also follows LVB-02. Resume affected processing only after the system owner and Operations confirm readiness, with Security clearance if an incident was involved.

### 3.2 Closing evidence
At close, each teller reconciles the till; an independent checker reviews the result and unresolved differences. Two authorized staff verify transfer into shared custody. The Branch Manager checks the daily exception log, outstanding complaints, unposted adjustments, and handover items. The closing checklist identifies each preparer and reviewer and the time of completion.

Unresolved differences remain visible overnight as open cases with an assigned owner. They are not a reason to falsify a balanced position. The manager sends the daily reconciliation summary to Operations by 17:00. If an incident prevents completion, send a status report by 17:00 identifying missing checks and the assigned recovery owner, then complete the evidence when safe.

### 3.3 Oversight and examples
Operations reviews branch exceptions every business day. Finance independently reviews monthly cash-reconciliation trends under LVB-08. Records of transactions, cash transfers, adjustments, and reconciliations are retained for seven years after the transaction date or relevant financial year-end, as specified by LVB-06 section 2.2.

Example: a CU 6,000 payout needs manager approval before release. Example: a CU 80 unresolved shortage must still be logged that day; the amount alone does not trigger the over-CU-100 escalation, but suspected fraud does. Example: a sole available staff member cannot open cash service, even if customers are waiting. The branch delays cash service and escalates the staffing issue.
