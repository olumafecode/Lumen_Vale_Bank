## 1. Protected concerns and reporting routes
### 1.1 Purpose and protection
LVB encourages honest reporting of suspected fraud, bribery, concealment of losses, customer mistreatment, falsified records, serious control failures, privacy misuse, and retaliation. A reporter need not prove wrongdoing before raising a reasonable concern. A concern that is not substantiated does not, by itself, imply dishonesty. Knowingly fabricated allegations may be addressed through the conduct process; honest mistakes remain protected.

Retaliation is prohibited against reporters, witnesses, people who assist an investigation, and staff who refuse an improper instruction. Examples include threats, unjustified shift changes, blocked training, or adverse treatment because a person raised a concern. Report suspected retaliation through any route below; it is assessed separately from the original allegation.

### 1.2 Available routes
Staff may use the restricted Speak-Up queue in CaseTrack, write to speakup@lumenvale.example, or request a private meeting with the Compliance Officer. Anonymous reporting is allowed through the fictional Speak-Up portal, which issues a case code for follow-up without requiring a name. These are scenario channels, not real addresses or services.

If the concern involves the Compliance Officer, CISO, People Operations lead, or CEO, use the Independent Speak-Up Reviewer through independent-review@lumenvale.example. If it involves the Board Audit Committee chair or the independence of the reviewer, route it to the non-conflicted Board Chair; if both are implicated, the non-conflicted directors appoint an external investigator. Never route a case to the person named in the allegation for intake approval.

### 1.3 Urgent issues and other complaints
Immediate safety or active security issues must also go to the appropriate emergency or on-call Security route; the Speak-Up acknowledgment period is not an emergency response time. Ordinary leave scheduling or expense disagreements normally go to People Operations or Finance. They may use Speak-Up if they involve misconduct, retaliation, concealment, or a conflict in the normal reporting route.

## 2. Intake, investigation, and updates
### 2.1 Information and acknowledgment
Provide the concern, approximate dates, people or functions involved, where relevant records may exist, and a safe way to communicate. Supply only records you are already authorized to access. Do not search a colleague's mailbox, remove customer files, or make covert copies to build a case. A reporter may choose not to provide an identity; anonymous reports are assessed on their available information.

The assigned intake officer acknowledges a report within two business days of receipt, using the chosen safe channel or anonymous case code. The acknowledgment explains the next step and reminds the reporter how to report retaliation. The officer records urgency, conflicts, safeguarding needs, and the assigned investigator in a restricted case. Identity information is stored separately from the general allegation summary.

### 2.2 Independent assessment
Within five business days of receipt, the intake officer completes an initial assessment and decides whether investigation, referral, further information, or closure is appropriate. An investigator must be independent of the subject and the disputed decision. Conflicted staff must not select the investigator, access witness identities, or approve findings. The investigator documents facts, evidence sources, interviews, and limits to the evidence without presuming guilt.

The bank protects identities on a need-to-know basis but cannot promise absolute confidentiality. If disclosure is necessary for a fair investigation or a required process, the case owner explains the proposed disclosure to the reporter where safe and practicable and records the rationale. Witnesses receive the same need-to-know protection.

### 2.3 Progress and conclusion
The case owner provides an update at least every ten business days while the case is open, even if the update only explains a justified delay. The internal target is to conclude within 30 business days of receipt. If this is not feasible, the owner records the reason, revised target, and next update date. The target does not justify skipping evidence or promising a particular outcome.

## 3. Outcomes, review, and records
### 3.1 Closing a case
The investigator distinguishes supported findings, unsupported allegations, and unresolved facts. The authorized decision maker approves corrective action independently of the investigation subject. The reporter receives a closure summary where contact is possible, explaining whether the concern was addressed and how to request review. The summary must not disclose another person's medical information, disciplinary details, or unrelated customer records.

The case owner records action owners and due dates and checks completion. A finding of no misconduct does not remove the duty to protect the reporter from retaliation. People Operations and the reviewer assess any reported adverse treatment without giving a line manager unnecessary access to the allegation. Temporary protective arrangements must not punish the reporter by default.

### 3.2 Review request
A reporter may request an independent review within ten business days of receiving a closure summary, identifying new evidence, an unmanaged conflict, or a material procedural concern. The Independent Speak-Up Reviewer appoints a non-conflicted reviewer who did not make the original finding. If that reviewer handled the original case, a non-conflicted Board Audit Committee member appoints another reviewer.

The review acknowledgment is due within two business days of receipt. The review outcome target is 15 business days; any extension requires a recorded explanation and an update to the reporter. Review can uphold the outcome, reopen fact-finding, or require corrective action. This internal route does not purport to limit rights or external routes that might exist in a real jurisdiction; none are defined by this synthetic corpus.

### 3.3 Record access and example
Speak-Up records are retained for seven years after case closure under LVB-06. Only assigned intake staff, independent investigators, and authorized reviewers receive case access. The Board Audit Committee receives quarterly de-identified statistics on case themes, timeliness, corrective actions, and retaliation concerns. Do not place report details in ordinary team minutes.

Example: a branch employee suspects a manager is suppressing cash discrepancies. The employee can report without the manager's permission and may use the anonymous route. They identify the dates and authorized records they observed; they do not enter other staff accounts. If a shift is changed as punishment, they report that as a separate retaliation concern linked to the case code.
