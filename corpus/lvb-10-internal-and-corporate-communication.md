## 1. Internal channels and message handling
### 1.1 Approved channels
Use bank email for formal internal correspondence, the approved staff chat for routine coordination, PolicyHub for current policies, StaffHub for leave and expenses, AccessDesk for access approvals, and the relevant restricted CaseTrack queue for incidents and investigations. A chat message can alert an owner that a request exists but does not replace approval in the required workflow.

Do not use personal email, personal messaging accounts, or public social-media groups for bank business involving Confidential or Restricted information. Classification follows LVB-02; a message with mixed data receives the highest applicable classification. Before sharing, check the recipient, business need, classification, and minimum necessary content. Restricted case identities must not appear in general team channels.

### 1.2 Response expectations and urgent messages
For ordinary internal email requiring action, acknowledge within two business days or set an out-of-office message naming an authorized alternate. This is an acknowledgment target, not a promise to complete every request in two days. The sender identifies the action, owner, and requested date. The recipient should clarify an unrealistic deadline and route the work appropriately.

Urgent security incidents use the on-call Security route within 15 minutes of discovery; do not rely on a manager reading email. Whistleblowing, privacy, and customer complaints use their own timelines. A general communication service target never extends a shorter policy deadline. If a restricted system is unavailable, use its documented fallback rather than an unapproved public channel.

### 1.3 Meetings and decisions
Record material decisions, approvals, action owners, and due dates in the approved system or team record. Do not include unnecessary medical, customer, or investigation details in minutes. A verbal instruction that needs formal approval is not effective until the required authorized record exists. Staff may ask for the written decision and escalate pressure to bypass this control through LVB-07 or LVB-03.

## 2. External statements and customer notices
### 2.1 Spokespeople and authorization
Only the CEO, Head of Corporate Communication, or a specifically designated written delegate may speak to the media on behalf of LVB. Employees who receive a media inquiry record the contact details and topic, refer it to Corporate Communication promptly, and make no substantive comment. Personal views must not be presented as bank statements, and confidentiality obligations still apply outside work.

Routine customer service may use approved templates within an employee's role. New public statements, marketing claims, and changes to standard customer notices need Corporate Communication and the relevant business-owner approval before release. Compliance reviews statements about products, obligations, or customer impact; the DPO reviews proposed personal-data disclosure. Do not invent product claims that are absent from approved material.

### 2.2 Incident and service communication
For a major incident, Corporate Communication prepares one controlled message using facts confirmed by the incident lead. Security verifies technical incident facts; Operations verifies service availability; the DPO and Compliance review privacy and reporting implications. The CEO or designated spokesperson approves the public release. No one should promise restoration times, absence of data loss, or compensation without an approved factual basis.

Branch staff may issue an already approved service-outage template after the Head of Operations confirms its applicability. If no template fits, escalate rather than drafting speculation. Any required customer or external notification decision is made by the authorized owners under LVB-02 and LVB-06. This policy supplies no legal notification timetable.

### 2.3 Publication checks
Before publishing, verify audience, facts, date, version, accessible wording, contact route, approval evidence, and absence of unnecessary personal data. Use only official accounts with assigned custodians and MFA. A second authorized person checks the final version against the approved text. Record who published, where, when, and which version. A draft in a shared folder is not publication approval.

## 3. Corrections, records, and examples
### 3.1 Errors and unauthorized disclosures
Report a materially inaccurate published statement to Corporate Communication immediately. Stop scheduled reuse where authorized, preserve the original version and approval history, and request a correction decision. Do not silently rewrite a significant claim so that the prior statement cannot be reconstructed. Corporate Communication coordinates a corrected version, publication time, audience, and explanation appropriate to the impact.

If the error discloses personal or Restricted information, also report it to Security within 15 minutes of discovery. Deleting a post or recalling an email does not remove the incident-reporting obligation. Security and the DPO assess the disclosure; Corporate Communication manages approved external wording. Staff must not contact all affected people independently before that coordinated decision.

### 3.2 Policy changes and records
Policy owners publish approved revisions through PolicyHub with version, effective date, owner, and change summary. Corporate Communication helps notify affected staff. An email summary must link to the authoritative version rather than become an untracked alternative policy. Staff acknowledge material policy updates within five business days of the request; managers follow up on overdue acknowledgments.

Routine internal communication is retained for two years after creation. Messages that support a transaction, employment decision, incident, or investigation inherit the relevant longer record schedule in LVB-06. Approved public statements and their approval packs are retained for seven years after publication as a specific communication record class. Holds suspend deletion. Corporate Communication owns this additional schedule and coordinates disposal with the DPO.

### 3.3 Examples and training
A journalist asks a teller whether a branch outage was a cyberattack. The teller records the inquiry and refers it to Corporate Communication, using only the approved customer-service message. A manager requests an expense approval by chat; the employee submits the StaffHub claim and obtains the required approval there. An employee sees an incorrect public fee claim; they report it immediately and preserve the published version rather than replacing it without review.

Staff receive communication and confidentiality guidance at induction. Official-account custodians receive role-specific publishing training before access is granted, and their access is reviewed quarterly. Departing custodians hand over account ownership through AccessDesk; they do not share their password with a successor.
