## 1. Professional conduct and customer treatment
### 1.1 Expected behavior
Staff must act honestly, treat customers and colleagues respectfully, follow approved controls, and keep accurate records. Discrimination, harassment, bullying, intimidation, retaliation, and deliberate deception are prohibited. A sales target, manager's instruction, or customer's request does not authorize falsifying a record, bypassing verification, or concealing a complaint.

Explain products only within the approved information available for the role. Do not promise approval, returns, fees, or rates not stated in authorized product material. The academic corpus does not contain lending criteria or interest rates, so the policy assistant must not invent them. Where a customer needs an answer outside the employee's authority, refer to the authorized product or service owner.

### 1.2 Confidentiality and account access
Use customer and employee records only for an assigned business purpose. Looking up a friend, relative, public figure, or colleague out of curiosity is prohibited even if system access technically allows it. Staff must not process their own account transactions or approve transactions for a close relative. Route such matters to an independent authorized colleague and record the conflict without sharing unnecessary personal detail.

Do not discuss Confidential or Restricted information in public spaces, personal messages, social media, or unapproved AI tools. Leaving the bank does not authorize disclosure of information obtained during employment. Data retention and legitimate disclosure follow LVB-06; confidentiality is not an excuse to hide evidence from an authorized independent investigation.

### 1.3 Fair handling of concerns
Managers listen to concerns without prejudging the outcome and refer specialist matters to the appropriate owner. Staff may challenge an instruction that appears inconsistent with policy and seek a written explanation. If the concern involves the manager, use an independent channel. Whistleblowing and retaliation protections in LVB-03 apply to good-faith reports, including reports that ultimately cannot be substantiated.

## 2. Gifts, outside interests, and conflicts
### 2.1 Gifts and hospitality
Cash, cash equivalents, personal loans from customers or suppliers, and gifts intended to influence a decision are prohibited regardless of value. A noncash gift or hospitality valued at CU 50 or less may be accepted only if occasional, unsolicited, unrelated to a pending decision, and unlikely to create obligation. Record every accepted item in the gift register within two business days.

An item worth more than CU 50 requires written Compliance Officer approval before acceptance. If advance approval is impossible, decline it or place it in bank custody pending a decision; do not use it personally. Once a giver's cumulative accepted value would exceed CU 100 in a calendar year, Compliance approval is required before that additional item is accepted, even if each item is individually below CU 50.

No gift or hospitality from a bidder may be accepted by a person participating in the active procurement decision, regardless of value. Compliance approval cannot override this prohibition. If the Compliance Officer is the recipient, the non-conflicted Head of People Operations and Board Audit Committee chair make the decision. An item suspected to be a bribe must be reported through LVB-03.

### 2.2 Conflicts and outside work
Disclose an actual, potential, or apparent conflict to the manager and Compliance Officer before participating in the affected decision. Examples include a relative's supplier bid, an ownership interest in a customer business, or outside paid work for a bank vendor. Declare relevant interests on joining, annually, and within five business days of a material change. An annual declaration does not remove the need to disclose before a specific decision.

Outside paid work requires manager and Compliance approval before it starts. They assess conflicts, confidentiality, working-time impact, and use of bank resources. Approval is not permission to use customer information or bank equipment for the outside role. A conflicted employee must step out of the decision until a documented management plan identifies an independent decision maker.

## 3. Investigation, action, and examples
### 3.1 Raising a conduct concern
An employee may raise ordinary conduct concerns with People Operations, the manager, or the Speak-Up route. Security incidents still require reporting within 15 minutes under LVB-02; do not wait for a conduct investigation. A privacy issue goes to the DPO as well as any applicable incident channel. People Operations records intake, checks conflicts, and assigns a fair process without asking a subject of the allegation to approve investigation of themselves.

### 3.2 Fair process and outcomes
The investigator gathers relevant information, distinguishes fact from inference, and gives the employee an appropriate opportunity to respond. Restrict information to people who need it for the process. Interim access restrictions may protect information or customers while facts are assessed; they are not a finding of guilt. People Operations records reasons and reviews continuing necessity.

Possible internal outcomes include no action, coaching, training, changes to duties, a formal warning, or separation after the authorized review. This list does not establish mandatory employment sanctions or real-world legal procedures. A manager must not impose an automatic penalty based solely on an allegation or make a payroll deduction outside an approved process. Separation coordination follows LVB-05 section 3.

An employee may request a review of a conduct decision through People Operations within ten business days of notification, identifying an error, new evidence, or procedural concern. A manager who made the original decision must not decide the review. People Operations acknowledges within three business days and records the reviewer and planned completion date. Whistleblowing review timelines are separately defined in LVB-03.

### 3.3 Training and examples
Staff complete conduct induction before customer-facing duties and annual refresher training. People Operations tracks completion; Compliance reviews the gift and conflict registers quarterly. Conduct records form part of the restricted personnel file, retained for six years after departure unless a longer case schedule or hold applies under LVB-06.

Example: a CU 40 meal from a supplier may still be prohibited during an active procurement decision. Example: three CU 40 gifts from one giver would total CU 120; the third requires prior Compliance approval even if no other prohibition applies. Example: an employee cannot view a relative's balance simply because the relative verbally asked them at a family gathering; the relative must use an authorized service process.
