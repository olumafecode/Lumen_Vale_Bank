## 1. Purpose, collection, and permitted use
### 1.1 Scope and principles
Personal data means information about an identifiable customer, employee, applicant, supplier contact, or other person in this fictional bank. Collect only information needed for an approved purpose, explain that purpose, keep data accurate, restrict access, and retain it only under the schedule in section 2.2. Do not reuse a customer record for an unrelated purpose merely because the bank already holds it.

The business owner documents the purpose and justification for each processing activity in the privacy register before launch. The DPO reviews new uses, sensitive datasets, automated decisions, and new suppliers before data is collected or transferred. This policy defines internal controls, not a legal basis under any real jurisdiction. Consent is not presumed from silence, and staff must not claim that every bank activity depends on consent.

### 1.2 Minimum necessary data
Use the approved form and avoid free-text copies of identity documents, medical details, or payment records. Explain required and optional fields. Do not collect full identity documents when an approved verification result is sufficient for the task. Customer onboarding follows the specific requirements in LVB-09; this minimization rule does not permit staff to omit required due-diligence evidence.

For training, testing, demonstrations, and this academic application, use synthetic records. A masked name does not necessarily anonymize a record if other fields identify the person. Do not put actual customer examples in the academic repository. Medical evidence goes only to the restricted People Operations channel; a line manager receives the operational absence information needed to arrange coverage.

### 1.3 Accuracy and changes
Staff who find an inaccurate record submit a correction request through the owning system rather than overwriting audit history. Verify a customer's identity before changing contact or identity information, and preserve the reason, source, date, and author of the update. Suspected identity misuse also goes to Compliance. The DPO resolves questions about a new purpose, excessive collection, or proposed external disclosure before the activity proceeds.

## 2. Sharing, retention, and disposal
### 2.1 Controlled sharing
Share personal data only with an authorized recipient for a recorded business purpose and through an approved encrypted channel. Check recipient identity, address, classification, and minimum content before sending. A colleague's seniority does not create a need to know. Suppliers need business-owner, Security, DPO, and Procurement review before receiving Confidential or Restricted information, consistent with LVB-02 section 3.2.

The DPO and Compliance Officer review external demands for records, including requests claiming official authority. Frontline staff must not disclose directly or promise a legal outcome. An accidental disclosure is a security incident: report within 15 minutes of discovery under LVB-02. A deletion request from a recipient does not replace an incident report.

### 2.2 Authoritative retention schedule
Customer due-diligence and account-opening records: seven years after the customer relationship ends. Transaction records and cash-transfer evidence: seven years after the transaction date. Financial reconciliations, manual-adjustment review packs, and expense records: seven years after the relevant financial year-end; LVB's financial year ends 31 December. Approved public statements and their approval packs: seven years after publication.

Personnel, leave, and offboarding records: six years after employee departure. Security incident, whistleblowing, privacy-request, and control-exception cases: seven years after case closure. Ordinary security logs: one year after creation. Access-management requests and access-review evidence: two years after request closure or review completion. Routine internal communication: two years after creation, unless it is evidence of a transaction, personnel decision, or case, in which case retain it with that record for the applicable longer period.

### 2.3 Holds and deletion
A legal, investigation, or audit hold suspends ordinary deletion for the records it covers. The hold owner records scope and release in CaseTrack; only written release permits normal disposal to resume. Owners review expired records quarterly, confirm holds, obtain a second-person check, and record secure deletion or approved destruction. Backups expire on a documented 90-calendar-day rolling cycle; deleted live records are not restored into routine use from backup. A hold requires preservation of relevant backup evidence beyond the normal cycle where necessary.

## 3. Privacy requests, assessment, and oversight
### 3.1 Request intake and verification
Anyone receiving a request to access, correct, or delete personal data must send it to the DPO's restricted Privacy queue by the end of the next business day. Staff must not refuse a request because it uses informal wording. Record the receipt time and request type using minimal data, and avoid sending full account files into the intake record.

The DPO acknowledges receipt within three business days of the bank's original receipt and verifies identity through an approved process before releasing information or making sensitive changes. The internal response target is 20 business days from original receipt. If identity or scope remains unresolved, the DPO explains the issue, records the reason for delay, and provides a revised target. These are invented internal service targets, not statutory time limits.

### 3.2 Review and response
The DPO checks scope, identity, third-party information, retention duties within this corpus, and any hold before deciding. A deletion request does not override an active hold or automatically erase a required record. If deletion is not possible, explain the applicable reason and any available restriction of use without claiming a legal exemption that the corpus does not define. Record what was searched, what was disclosed or changed, the decision, and reviewer approval.

For a possible personal-data incident, Security notifies the DPO within one hour of receiving the incident report. The DPO assesses data types, people affected, safeguards, likely impact, and appropriate response with Compliance. External notification decisions are documented; this corpus does not establish regulator names, legal thresholds, or statutory breach deadlines.

### 3.3 Oversight and example
The DPO reviews the privacy register and open requests quarterly and reports overdue actions to the Board Risk Committee. New high-impact processing requires a documented privacy-impact review before launch, including necessity, affected people, data flows, risks, mitigations, and accountable owner. An unresolved high risk goes to the DPO and relevant executive for a recorded stop-or-proceed decision.

Example: a former customer asks for immediate deletion of onboarding documents one year after account closure. The DPO verifies identity and explains the seven-year post-relationship schedule. Staff do not promise immediate erasure or disclose records before verification. Any active hold is checked separately and can require retention beyond the scheduled period.
